**Sunhaven concept-to-game detail review**

Reviewed September 7, 2026 against the current 1.8.1 source, the three Sunhaven concepts, saved 1.8.0 district captures, and saved 1.8.1 polish captures and performance evidence. This is an assessment and proposed implementation sequence; no game changes or new runtime benchmarks were performed for this review.

The concepts establish a coherent, appealing target. The largest gap is the environment content: building shapes, convincing surface construction, layered landforms, vegetation variety, and composition at player height. The game already has useful rendering foundations. Reaching substantially closer to the references requires a substantial environment-art and asset-pipeline pass, with targeted rendering corrections.

Evidence reviewed: [docks concept](sunhaven-docks-v1.png), [village concept](sunhaven-village-v1.png), [island concept](sunhaven-island-v1.png), [saved Detailed docks](../../tests/artifacts/sunhaven/docks-high.png), [saved village](../../tests/artifacts/sunhaven/village.png), and [1.8.1 boathouse close-up](../../tests/artifacts/sunhaven-polish/after-door.png). The wide district captures predate the 1.8.1 geometry fixes; the current source and newer close-up confirm that the underlying asset style remains applicable to this comparison.

| Area | What makes the concept work | Current implementation and required change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buildings | Deep door openings, roof overhangs, substantial trim, visible siding, roof tiles, porches, and varied silhouettes | `openHouse` and `cabin` assemble mostly box surfaces with thin decorative strips. Build a reusable coastal architecture kit with modeled edges, recesses, siding profiles, awnings, roof options, and modest interior detail. Give the boathouse its own silhouette. |
| Docks and cargo | Individual boards with end grain and seams; thick posts, braces, metal fasteners, rope, worn crates, shaped boats | Decks are large slabs with navy seam strips; boats are scaled faceted balls with box seats. Author boardwalk modules, real hull shapes, support frames, and a small cargo/rope/fixture kit. Use geometry where it changes the silhouette and textures for fine wear. |
| Material detail | Distinct painted wood, bare wood, metal, stone, fabric, and plastic; consistent detail scale | Building and dock wood uses a 128×128 procedural color tile, with no matching normal or roughness maps. Replace it with coordinated material sets and well-scaled UVs. Existing photographed nature maps can remain where their style fits. |
| Terrain and coast | Rock shoulders, ledges, broken banks, small beaches, and irregular transitions that frame routes | The map is primarily a smooth heightfield with flattened pads and a mathematical inlet. Author local terrain shapes and add fitted cliff, shoreline, and retaining-wall meshes. Keep walkable surfaces and collision aligned. |
| Vegetation | Broad oak crowns, shrubs, flowers, tussocks, and small plants grouped around rock and building edges | The game has six procedural tree templates, grass, and ferns, but the composition is predominantly trees scattered across a broad grass surface. Add a small family of shrubs, flowers, coastal grass, and spreading oaks; place them in deliberate groups. |
| Lighting and sky | Clear blue sky, substantial soft clouds, warm sunlight, shaded recesses, and objects visibly grounded against their surroundings | Sun, sky environment lighting, tone mapping, moving shadows, and a Detailed contact pass already exist. Tune their balance against finished assets; add baked crevice shading and, where useful, static indirect lighting. Improve cloud shapes and scale. |
| Water | Clear shallows over a varied seabed, coastline reflections, foam at irregular edges, and wet materials near the surface | Refraction, reflection, animated normals, depth tinting, and foam already exist. Correct inlet visibility checks, dress the seabed, and extend wetness/foam to rocks and pilings. |
| Foreground character and builds | Smooth helmet and limbs, convincing cloth and straps, beveled wooden builds and metal connectors | The articulated actor and construction pieces already communicate the intended identity. A later mesh/material pass should bring their surface finish and silhouettes into line with the improved environment. Preserve the existing animation and building behavior. |

Three concrete technical issues deserve attention during the first area upgrade:

1. **Water quality is gated by the direction away from the island center.** In `nature-water.js`, `refractCoast` rejects views whose camera-position/direction dot product is below -8; `reflectCoast` uses the complementary outward-facing condition. A camera can look inland across visible inlet water and fail these tests. That is especially relevant to the docks reference. Replace the radial approximation with a conservative test for visible water regions, including the inlet, and validate both inland-facing and sea-facing cameras. This is a source-level finding; the exact new behavior still needs browser verification. The saved tour's docks snapshot also records zero accumulated refraction passes, which reinforces the need for targeted validation without proving that all dock views fail.

2. **The current batching helper discards useful authored attributes.** `materials.js:batchDecorations` retains only position, normal, and the first UV set. An imported model or new generated mesh using vertex colors, additional UVs for baked shading, or authored tangents would lose those attributes if sent through that path. Extend batching to preserve the attributes each material needs and merge only compatible geometry. The existing separation between visible static geometry and collision is a useful foundation to retain.

3. **Foliage optimization needs to grow with the art.** Leaf batches have frustum culling disabled, while distance changes their detail level without removing distant trees. Branches are separate meshes per tree. Introduce spatial foliage batches with correct bounds, shared branch instances where appropriate, distant canopy representations, and selective shadow detail. Verify bounds against both the main camera and water reflection camera. The current code supports identifying these opportunities, but profiling is still required to determine their relative cost.

For asset production, use a reusable kit with authored meshes and material maps. GLB is a practical import path, while suitably detailed procedural geometry can also supply repeatable modules. Establish consistent units, origins, texture density, material names, collision proxies, and near/mid/far detail variants. Three.js provides the relevant [glTF loader](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html), [physically based material maps](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html), and [mesh instancing](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html); the assessment does not identify a feature gap that requires changing engines.

Use shared texture sheets for repeated trim and props. Spend resolution on objects viewed close to the camera, and establish the actual texture-memory budget with the first scene. Keep the teal/coral/cream/navy palette consistent across material sets. A realistic wood texture alone will not supply roof overhangs, board thickness, or a boat's hull shape.

The current offline package embeds image data in JavaScript and generates geometry at startup. An asset kit adds loading, preparation, and packaging work: import/preprocess the meshes, retain the required material attributes, track load failures, manage shared resource lifetimes, and include every dependency in the portable build. Preserve direct-file launch by loading embedded model data or by using an explicitly tested packaging approach. If compressed GPU textures are introduced, [KTX2Loader](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/KTX2Loader.html) needs its transcoder dependencies packaged locally too. Larger source images and external model URLs alone would not satisfy the existing offline contract.

The concepts also need a consistent interpretation. The overview and original prompts emphasize flat, playable roofs, while the close village image contains prominent pitched roofs and taller facades. Use pitched roofs on suitable landmarks and non-playable sections, retaining intentional flat decks where rooftop building is part of the map. Treat the aerial image as a composition reference rather than an exact scale drawing. Preserve clear doors, both permanent dock exits, buildable pockets, and useful sightlines as detail is added. Major visible obstacles need matching collision and cover behavior; small flowers, fasteners, and surface wear should not create movement snags.

Recommended implementation order:

1. **Establish a dock reference scene.** Select repeatable cameras looking across the inlet, toward the boathouse, along the boardwalk, and down at shoreline transitions. Include ordinary gameplay views and close inspections. Record the existing appearance and per-frame cost at fixed resolution and settings.
2. **Create the first reusable kit.** Finish one distinctive boathouse, boardwalk/post/rail modules, two crate variants, one shaped boat, several shoreline rocks, an oak, shrubs/flowers, and a few fixtures. Build the necessary material/import/batching support alongside these assets. Keep the first area small enough to iterate thoroughly.
3. **Compose and light the complete area.** Shape the bank, arrange the rock-to-shrub-to-flower transitions, add shallow-water detail, correct water visibility, and tune lighting with the new materials in place. Review moving footage as well as stills so shimmer, popping, reflection changes, and traversal remain visible.
4. **Prove gameplay and performance.** Walk both exits, enter and leave the boathouse, build across designated platforms, test camera clearance and bot navigation, and inspect collision around new cover. Measure representative traversal and combat with ten bots and player builds. Check all three graphics presets and the offline package.
5. **Expand using the validated kit.** Carry the material language and construction modules into the village, then the lighthouse terraces, orchard, and quarry. Add district-specific assets where they improve recognition. Finish the foreground actor and build-piece materials once the environment establishes the quality standard.

For performance, the existing [1.8.1 report](../../tests/artifacts/sunhaven-polish/performance-after.json) records a fixed-view Detailed Sunhaven result of roughly 175 FPS, 719 draw calls, and 4.14 million rendered triangles on an RTX 5090 at a 1600×1000 viewport. These are saved measurements, not a new benchmark. Renderer counters accumulate across the frame's passes, so the triangle count is not a count of unique scene geometry. The frame rate is display-capped and cannot establish ordinary-laptop headroom. Profile CPU time, GPU time where available, draw calls, texture memory, loading time, and frame-time spikes in the upgraded dock scene before selecting whole-island density budgets. A proposed 60 FPS target corresponds to about 16.7 ms per frame; target hardware still needs to be specified and measured.

The first milestone should deliver a playable dock area whose architecture, material scale, shoreline layering, and composition visibly approach the reference at ordinary camera distance. That gives the rest of the map a concrete visual standard and a measured implementation cost.
